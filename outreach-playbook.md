# seefood.com — Outreach Scripts

Ground rules that make every message below work:
- **Slow replies win.** Wait 24–48h before answering anything. Eagerness = money.
- **Never negotiate against yourself.** One number per message, max. Never improve an offer they haven't rejected.
- **Precise numbers, not round ones.** $2,750 reads "this is all I have." $3,000 reads "opening bid."
- **Always leave an easy exit.** "No hard feelings" lines get replies from people who'd otherwise ghost.
- **Never mention:** startup, app, investors, Silicon Valley (the show), or any launch plans.
- **Stay vague, never lie.** "Small food project" is true. Fabricated identities or sob stories unwind deals and cross legal lines.

---

## 1. First contact (email, plain Gmail)

**Subject:** seefood.com

> Hi [Name],
>
> I came across seefood.com and saw it's been sitting quiet for a long while. I'm working on a small food-related project and the name made me smile.
>
> Any chance you'd consider letting it go to someone who'd actually use it? Happy to keep things simple.
>
> Kyle

*Why it works: no numbers (they anchor first), no company smell, flatters the pun, tiny ask.*

## 2. Follow-up — 12 days later, same thread

> Hi [Name] — floating this back up in case it got buried.
>
> No pressure either way. If you'd rather hang onto it, just tell me and I won't bother you again.
>
> Kyle

*The "tell me no and I disappear" line is the single best reply-generator with dormant owners.*

## 4. They reply: "How much are you offering?"

Deflect once — force them to anchor:

> Honestly, I don't know what's fair for a name that's been sitting since the '90s — did you have a number in mind?
>
> I'll be straight with you: I'm one person, not a company with a domain budget, so if we're in totally different ballparks I'd rather not waste your time.

---

## 5. They won't name a number — you open

> Would $1,500 get it done?
>
> That's real money for something that's been idle, and I can make it painless — Escrow.com, I cover all fees, and since it's at Namecheap the transfer is a 5-minute push. Done this week.

*Speed + zero friction is worth thousands. "Done this week" closes fence-sitters.*

---

## 6. They anchor high ($20k+)

> That's beyond me, unfortunately — I'm not one of the big domain investors, just a guy who liked the pun.
>
> What I could actually do is $4,250 cash, or if the number matters more than the timing, $2,000 now plus $250/month for 24 months through escrow — that gets you closer to your figure.
>
> If neither works, genuinely no hard feelings. I'll leave you my number in case anything ever changes: [cell]

*Flinch + two structures + open door. The lease-to-own option lets them "win" the bigger number.*

---

## 7. They're in range ($3–8k) — close it

> Deal at $[split the difference once] if we can wrap it this week.
>
> I'll open the Escrow.com transaction today and cover the fee. You accept, push the domain at Namecheap, money releases. Five minutes of your time.

*One concession, then close. Do not reopen terms.*

---

## 8. Stalled — the trade bag (in order of what actually works)

Deploy one at a time, not as a menu:

> What if we did $[cash] plus a "Founding Friend" credit on the site permanently — you were part of this name's story for 29 years, that shouldn't vanish.

> Another thought: $[cash] now, plus another $10,000 if the project ever raises serious outside money. One-page agreement, your lawyer can look at it.

> I'd also happily make you an informal advisor — you clearly had good taste in names before most people had email.

## 9. Six-month re-approach (if all dead)

>

*The credible deadline ("about to buy the alternative") is the strongest honest lever there is.*

## Never do
- Fake identities, fabricated stories, or a shell buyer with a false backstory (fraud territory).
- Trademark or UDRP threats — they predate you by decades; it's reverse domain hijacking and kills the deal forever.
- Launch or tweet anything "SeeFood" before the deal closes.
